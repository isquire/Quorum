How to use Quorum

A step-by-step work process for running Connection Church Board of Administration meetings under Robert’s Rules of Order.

1. What this app does

Quorum is the board’s shared cockpit for meetings. It keeps a single, accurate record of every parliamentary action — roll call, agenda progress, motions, votes, reports, attachments — so no one is scribbling minutes in parallel and no one has to argue later about what actually happened.

The headline promise: if you run the meeting through the app, the minutes are written automatically. Every click on the live meeting screen produces a timestamped minutes entry. You review and approve those at the next meeting.

2. Roles & who does what

Quorum has roles aligned with the church’s Bylaws (Art. VII). An admin creates every user and assigns a role.

Role What they can do
admin Create, edit, remove, and reset passwords for any user. Manages archiving and the admin action log. Admins can do everything a pastor can do.
pastor President of the corporation. Chairs all meetings of the assembly, Board of Deacons, and Board of Administration. Schedules meetings, builds agendas, drives the live meeting, approves minutes and reports.
deacon Member of the Board of Deacons and Board of Administration. Advisory to the Pastor, examines membership applicants. Votes on all board matters.
trustee Member of the Board of Administration only. Custodian of church property. Votes on Board of Admin matters.
secretary Keeps minutes of all official meetings, custodian of legal documents, maintains the membership roll. Takes roll call, adds notes to the minutes timeline, edits minutes entries before approval.
treasurer Handles all church finances, submits treasurer reports. Serves both boards.
assistant_treasurer Performs the Treasurer’s duties in their absence. Same permissions as Treasurer.
member General church member. Can make motions, second motions, and cast votes when voting is open at assembly meetings.

A user is “voting” if their profile has Voting member checked. Admins may be non-voting (e.g. the pastor’s assistant). Legacy roles chair and vice_chair are still recognized and carry the same privileges as pastor.

3. Before the meeting

  1. Chair schedules the meeting. Meetings → New meeting. Pick the meeting type (regular, special, annual, emergency), date/time, and location. Save.
  2. Chair (or secretary) builds the agenda. Open the meeting and use the agenda editor to add items. Each item gets a category that maps to the Robert’s Rules order of business:
    • Minutes approval — approve last meeting’s minutes.
    • Report — one per committee / treasurer report.
    • Unfinished business — carried over from last time.
    • New business — anything new requiring board action.
    • Announcement — FYI items, no vote needed.
    Drag items to reorder them. Assign a presenter if someone specific is walking the board through it.
  3. Attach background documents. Upload the treasurer’s spreadsheet, the proposed budget PDF, or any supporting docs and attach them to the meeting or a specific agenda item. Anyone signed in can download; nothing is ever served as a public static file.
  4. Treasurer & committees file their reports. Reports → New report. Write the report in markdown, link it to the meeting, and submit. The chair will approve it during the meeting.

4. Starting the meeting

  1. Everyone logs in and opens the meeting page. The chair clicks Start meeting → status becomes in progress, stage becomes Call to order, and the minutes timeline gets its first entry: “Chair called the meeting to order.”
  2. Chair (or vice-chair) clicks Advance stage to move to Roll call.
  3. The secretary works down the attendance list, toggling each member Present as they arrive. The Quorum badge in the top-left turns green the instant the threshold is met (more than half of voting members). If you don’t have quorum you cannot conduct business — the board can only agree to recess or adjourn.
  4. Once quorum is met, chair advances to Minutes approval and approves the prior meeting’s minutes.

5. Working through the agenda

The chair advances through the order of business one stage at a time. Within each stage, click an agenda item in the left pane to make it the “current item” — the minutes timeline records the transition, and any motion made while that item is current is linked to it.

  • Reports: the presenter walks the board through their report (which is already filed in the app). Chair opens the floor for questions.
  • Unfinished / new business: chair invites motions on each item. This is where most of the action happens.
  • Announcements: FYI only. No votes.

You cannot advance out of a business stage while a motion is still on the floor — Quorum will block the click and flash a warning. Dispose of the motion first (vote, withdraw, or table).

6. Making & voting on motions

This is the heart of Robert’s Rules. Quorum walks every motion through the same lifecycle:

  1. Make. Any voting member clicks Make a motion, types the motion text (“I move that we…”), and submits. Status: proposed.
  2. Second. A different voting member clicks Second. A maker cannot second their own motion — Quorum enforces this. Status: seconded.
  3. Debate. The chair recognizes members to speak for or against. There’s no timer in the app; the chair runs the debate verbally.
  4. Open vote. Chair clicks Open vote. Status: voting. Every voting member now sees Yes / No / Abstain buttons.
  5. Cast votes. Members click their choice. The live tally updates in real time. Members can change their vote up until the chair closes the vote.
  6. Close vote. Chair clicks Close vote. The result is computed (simple or two-thirds majority, depending on the motion type — abstentions never count toward the total) and written to the minutes as: “Motion by A. Smith to X — PASSED 4–1–1.” Status: passed or failed.

The chair votes only to make or break a tie, per Robert’s Rules. The app lets the chair vote any time — abstain unless breaking a tie.

7. Amendments

Amendments are nested under the main motion. To propose one:

  1. A member selects Amendment from the motion-type dropdown while a main motion is on the floor.
  2. The amendment goes through its own propose → second → debate → vote cycle.
  3. Robert’s Rules says the innermost amendment is voted first. Quorum handles this automatically — the motion card always shows the deepest unresolved amendment as the active motion. Once it’s voted on, the app pops back to the parent.
  4. After all amendments are resolved, the board votes on the (possibly amended) main motion.

Withdrawing: the maker of a motion may withdraw it before the vote opens. Withdrawn motions are kept in the minutes with a withdrawn marker.

8. Adjournment

  1. Chair advances through the remaining stages until reaching Announcements.
  2. A member moves to adjourn and another seconds. This can be handled as a formal motion or, in practice for a board meeting, the chair simply clicks Adjourn.
  3. Status becomes adjourned, the adjournment timestamp is logged, and the meeting is frozen on the live page. The minutes timeline is the final record of what happened.

9. After the meeting

  1. Secretary reviews the minutes. Minutes → [meeting]. The timeline is reverse chronological; every entry can be edited to fix wording until the chair approves them. Edited entries are flagged.
  2. Export as plain text. Use the Export button to download a .txt copy for distribution or filing.
  3. Chair approves at the next meeting. During the next meeting’s Minutes approval stage, the chair clicks Approve minutes. After approval the timeline is locked — the record is now the board’s official account.

10. Reports & attachments

  • Treasurer reports and committee reports live under Reports. Create one, write markdown, attach any backing documents, link it to the meeting it’ll be presented at, and submit.
  • Chair approves each report (typically during the report’s agenda item).
  • Attachments can be attached to a meeting, an agenda item, or a report. Allowed types: PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, plain text / markdown, CSV, and images. Max size: 25 MB.
  • Every download is auth-gated. Logged-out users cannot fetch files even if they have the URL.

11. Quorum rules by body

Different bodies have different quorum rules per the church’s Constitution and Bylaws. The app enforces these automatically via the quorum badge on the live meeting screen.

Body Quorum rule Bylaws source
Assembly (annual or special business meeting) One-third or more of active members present Constitution Art. VIII §4
Board of Deacons Majority of Deacons present, provided all members have been notified Bylaws Art. I §2
Board of Administration Majority of Board members present, provided all members have been notified Bylaws Art. I §3

“Active member” is tracked per-user in the admin panel. The secretary should update the Active member checkbox during periodic membership roll revisions per Bylaws Art. III §5. Any member whose Active member flag is unchecked is excluded from the quorum denominator for assembly meetings.

If the quorum badge is red, the meeting cannot conduct substantive business under Robert’s Rules. The body may only recess, set a future meeting date, or adjourn.

12. The two-board model

Per the church’s Constitution (Art. VII §2), governance is split across two boards and the general assembly:

BodyMembersChair
Board of Deacons Pastor + ≥3 Deacons Pastor
Board of Administration All Deacons + ≥3 Trustees + Secretary + Treasurer + Asst. Treasurer Pastor
Assembly All active church members Pastor

Every Deacon is automatically a member of the Board of Administration. Trustees sit only on the Board of Administration. When creating a meeting, choose the correct board from the dropdown — attendance rolls and quorum calculations are scoped accordingly.

Family restriction: Constitution Art. VII §2.5 states that no two members of the same immediate family may serve on the same board. The admin panel tracks this via the “Family group” field on each user.

13. Deacons-only votes & supermajority

Deacons-only votes (Constitution Art. VIII §6)

If business pertaining to the unique responsibilities of the Board of Deacons is transacted at a Board of Administration meeting, only the Pastor and Deacons may vote. The chair (or motion maker) ticks the Deacons-only vote checkbox when making the motion. Trustees will see a yellow banner and cannot cast a vote; the app returns 403 if they attempt it.

Supermajority motion types

Three motion types automatically require a two-thirds majority:

Motion typeRequired majorityBylaws source
Bylaw amendment2/3 of members presentBylaws Art. IX
Property transfer2/3 of membershipBylaws Art. VI §2
Pastor election2/3 of votes cast (secret ballot)Bylaws Art. II §1

These motion types appear in the motion-type dropdown only during assembly meetings (annual or special business). The app pre-sets the majority to two-thirds and cannot be overridden.

14. Notification requirement

Bylaws Art. I §§2–3 state that quorum for both the Board of Deacons and the Board of Administration requires all members to have been notified. The quorum badge will stay red until every attendance row shows a “notified” timestamp.

The secretary (or chair) marks each member notified by clicking the ! button next to their name in the attendance panel. This is typically done before or at the start of the meeting.

Assembly meetings (annual/special business) do not have a notification requirement in the quorum check — but notice must still be given on the two Sundays prior per Constitution Art. VIII §§2–3.

15. Acting chair

Bylaws Art. I §2: if the Pastor is absent or the assembly is temporarily without a Pastor, the Board of Deacons selects a temporary chairman from its membership. This person also chairs the Board of Administration if needed.

When creating or editing a meeting, select an Acting chair from the dropdown. This grants that person all chair-equivalent powers for that meeting only: calling to order, advancing stages, opening/closing votes, and adding notes.

The acting chair’s name appears on the quorum badge and next to the meeting title on the live page.

16. Vote methods

When making a motion, the maker selects one of two vote methods. The method determines how votes are captured in the app:

Voice / show-of-hands

Members vote verbally or by raising hands. The chair (or secretary) counts and enters the totals directly into the app using the manual tally form — three fields for Yes, No, and Abstain. Individual members do not vote through the app.

This is the default method and the right choice for most board motions, since only the Pastor, Secretary, and a few board members are typically using devices during the meeting.

Roll call (per-member)

Each voting member casts their vote individually through the app by clicking Yes / No / Abstain. The app records who voted which way, providing a full audit trail. Use this when the bylaws require a roll-call vote or when the board wants individual votes on the record.

The minutes entry for each vote includes a label — [voice vote] or [roll call] — so the record clearly shows which method was used.

If a member tries to cast a per-member vote on a voice-method motion, the app will redirect them with a warning. The chair enters the tally instead.

17. Service terms & term limits

The app tracks term limits and service timelines for the four elected board positions. These positions are voted on at the annual business meeting and begin service the month after the meeting (e.g. elected in January → term starts February 1).

Term rules

Position Term length Max consecutive terms Cooldown
Deacon 3 years 1 1 year off before eligible again
Trustee 3 years 1 1 year off before eligible again
Treasurer 2 years 3 (Bylaws Art. II §2) 1 year off before eligible again
Asst. Treasurer 2 years 2 1 year off before eligible again

Positions not listed above — Pastor, Secretary — have no term limits and are not tracked in the term system.

How to use the Boards page

Navigate to Boards in the top menu to see all three governing bodies. Click a board to see:

  • Current service terms — who is serving, their role, term start/end dates, months remaining, and which consecutive term they are on.
  • Expiring soon — terms ending within 6 months, highlighted so the board can plan ahead for elections.
  • In cooldown — members who have served the maximum consecutive terms and must wait before being eligible again. Shows the eligibility date and months remaining.
  • Past terms — completed, resigned, or removed terms for historical reference.

Recording a new term

After an election at the annual business meeting, an admin clicks Record new term on the board detail page:

  1. Select the member who was elected.
  2. Choose their role (Deacon, Trustee, Treasurer, or Asst. Treasurer).
  3. Select the annual business meeting where the election occurred. The Term start date auto-populates to the first of the month after the meeting. The Term end date is calculated automatically from the term length.
  4. Add any notes (optional) and save.

The app warns if the member is still in a cooldown period but does not block the recording — the admin can override if needed.

Ending a term early

If a member resigns or is removed mid-term, click End early next to their term. Enter the last day of service, select the reason (Resigned or Removed), and optionally add notes. The term moves to the past-terms history with the appropriate status.

Dashboard

The main dashboard shows a Terms expiring soon card when any terms are ending within 6 months, so officers can plan ahead for nominations and elections.

18. Timezone

All timestamps in Quorum are displayed in Eastern Time (ET), which automatically handles the switch between Eastern Standard Time (EST) and Eastern Daylight Time (EDT).

  • Meeting times, minutes timestamps, report submissions, and all other dates/times appear in Eastern time.
  • When scheduling meetings, enter times in Eastern time — what shows on your clock in Detroit.
  • Exported minutes text files also use Eastern time labels.

19. Kiosk check-in mode

For larger meetings (especially congregational assemblies), Quorum offers a kiosk mode — a tablet-friendly, full-screen check-in page where attendees walk up and mark themselves present while an official observes.

Opening the kiosk
  1. From the meeting detail page (or the live chair view), click Open kiosk. You must be a secretary, chair, or pastor to see this button.
  2. A new browser tab opens with the kiosk check-in URL. This URL works without requiring a login — it uses a secure, time-limited token.
  3. Open this URL on a tablet or a second screen positioned where attendees can reach it. The link is valid for 12 hours.
Using the kiosk (for attendees)
  1. Type your name in the search box, or tap a letter button to filter the list.
  2. Find your name card and tap it. A green confirmation appears briefly.
  3. Step aside for the next person — the kiosk resets automatically.
How it works
  • Each check-in is logged to the meeting minutes with a “(kiosk check-in)” notation.
  • The quorum count updates in real time on both the kiosk header and the chair’s live view.
  • The kiosk can only mark people present — it cannot mark anyone absent. Only the secretary or chair can reverse attendance from the live view.
  • The kiosk link is cryptographically signed and tied to one specific meeting. It cannot be used to access any other part of the app.

20. Confidential minutes

Some agenda topics — staff salaries, personnel matters, personal financial decisions — are sensitive. Quorum lets officers mark agenda items as confidential so that non-officers receive a redacted copy of the minutes.

Marking an agenda item confidential

When adding or editing an agenda item, check the Confidential checkbox. A confidential badge appears next to the item on the agenda page.

Any minutes entry recorded while a confidential agenda item is the current item — motions, votes, chair notes — is automatically marked confidential. You do not need to flag each entry individually.

Standalone confidential notes

The chair or secretary can also mark an individual note as confidential using the Confidential checkbox on the chair/secretary note form in the live meeting view, regardless of the current agenda item.

Who can see confidential content?

Officers — admin, pastor, chair, vice-chair, and secretary — see the full text of every minutes entry, with a confidential badge marking sensitive items.

All other members (deacons, trustees, treasurers, general members) see a redacted placeholder: [CONFIDENTIAL — content restricted to officers] in place of the entry text.

Exported minutes

The plain-text export (Export button on the minutes page) applies the same redaction rules. Officers get the full text; non-officers get redacted entries plus a notice at the top that some proceedings have been redacted.

Live meeting stream

During the live meeting, all participants (including officers) see every entry in the minutes stream with a confidential badge on flagged entries. Redaction is applied only after the meeting, when members view or export the minutes.

21. Archiving meetings & reports

Admins can archive meetings and reports to remove them from the normal views without permanently deleting them. This is useful for cleaning up test meetings, cancelled items, or records that no longer need to appear in day-to-day lists.

How to archive
  • On the Meetings list page, each past meeting row has an Archive button (admin only). You can also archive from the meeting detail page.
  • On the Reports list page, each report row has an Archive button (admin only). You can also archive from the report detail page.
  • A confirmation prompt appears before archiving.
Viewing archived items

On the Meetings or Reports list page, click View archived (top-right) to see all archived items. From this view, click Restore to move an item back into the active list. Only admins can see this toggle.

What archiving affects
  • Archived meetings are hidden from the dashboard, meetings list, and minutes list.
  • Archived reports are hidden from the dashboard and reports list.
  • Archived items are not deleted — all data (minutes, motions, votes, attachments) is preserved.
  • Direct links to archived meetings and reports still work for anyone who has the URL.
  • Every archive and restore action is recorded in the admin action log.

22. Admin action log

Every admin action in the system is recorded in a permanent audit log. Admins can view the log by clicking Admin log in the top navigation bar.

What is logged
  • User management: creating, editing, removing, restoring, and resetting passwords for users.
  • Archiving and restoring meetings and reports.
Log details

Each entry shows the timestamp, which admin performed the action, the action type, the target (e.g. user #5, meeting #12), and a human-readable description. The log is sorted newest-first and paginated at 50 entries per page.

The admin log is read-only — entries cannot be edited or deleted. This ensures a reliable audit trail for governance purposes.

23. Tips & pitfalls

  • One browser per person. Every attending board member should have Quorum open on their own device so they can second motions and cast their own votes.
  • Don’t forget to open the vote. A common slip-up: chair discusses a motion and then tries to advance without opening the vote. The motion stays on the floor and blocks advancement.
  • Abstentions don’t count in the denominator. A motion passes simple majority if yes > no; two-thirds if yes × 3 ≥ (yes + no) × 2. Abstentions are recorded but don’t affect the math.
  • Use chair/secretary notes for anything the app can’t capture structurally (“Pastor joined by phone at 7:18”). They become timestamped minutes entries.
  • Fix wording before approval. The secretary can edit any minutes entry until the chair approves them at the next meeting. After approval, the record is frozen.
  • If the quorum badge is red, stop. Robert’s Rules does not let you conduct substantive business without quorum. You can recess, set a future date, or adjourn.

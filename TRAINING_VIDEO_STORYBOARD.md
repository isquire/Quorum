# Quorum Training Video — Storyboard & Narration Script

> **Purpose:** Step-by-step guide for producing a training video that shows
> church officers how to use the Quorum application for board meetings.
>
> **Estimated total runtime:** 25–35 minutes
>
> **Recommended format:** Screen-recording with voice-over narration.
> Record each scene separately so you can re-record individual sections
> without re-doing the whole video.

# PART 1 — INTRODUCTION & SETUP

---

## Scene 1: Title Card & Welcome

**Duration:** ~30 seconds

### On Screen
- Static title card with church logo / Quorum branding.
- Text: *"Quorum — Training Video for Connection Church Officers"*
- Fade to the presenter or directly to the login screen.

### Narration

> "Welcome to the Quorum training video. Quorum is the board's shared
> cockpit for running meetings at Connection Church — Board of
> Administration, Board of Deacons, and congregational assemblies.
>
> In the next thirty minutes, we'll walk through everything you need to
> know — from scheduling your first meeting to approving the minutes
> afterward. Let's get started."

---

## Scene 2: What Quorum Does — The Big Picture

**Duration:** ~1 minute

### On Screen
- Show the Guide page (`/guide`) — scroll slowly through the overview
  and the Quick Reference flowchart at the bottom.

### Narration

> "Before we dive into the buttons, let's talk about what Quorum actually
> does and why we use it.
>
> Quorum keeps a single, accurate record of every parliamentary action:
> roll call, agenda progress, motions, votes, reports, and attachments.
> Nobody has to scribble minutes in parallel. Nobody has to argue later
> about what happened.
>
> Here's the headline promise: **if you run the meeting through the app,
> the minutes are written automatically.** Every click on the live meeting
> screen creates a timestamped entry. You review and approve those minutes
> at the next meeting.
>
> The app follows Robert's Rules of Order and enforces our church's
> Constitution and Bylaws — quorum thresholds, two-thirds supermajority
> votes, deacons-only restrictions, term limits — all built in."

### Production Note
- Consider overlaying a simple animated flowchart:
  `Schedule → Build agenda → Start meeting → Roll call → Motions & votes → Adjourn → Review minutes → Approve`

---

## Scene 3: Logging In & the Dashboard

**Duration:** ~1 minute 30 seconds

### On Screen
1. Open the browser and navigate to the Quorum URL.
2. Show the login page — type an email and password, click **Log in**.
3. Land on the **Dashboard**.
4. Point out the navigation bar: Dashboard, Meetings, Governance, Admin (if admin), Guide.
5. Highlight dashboard cards: upcoming meetings, recent minutes, expiring terms.

### Narration

> "Open your browser and go to the Quorum address your admin gave you.
> You'll see the login page. Enter the email and password your
> administrator set up for you, and click Log In.
>
> You land on your personalized dashboard. What you see here depends on
> your role.
>
> [Mouse over the stat cards]
> Admins see stat cards at the top — total users, active users, upcoming
> meetings, and pending reports — plus a feed of recent admin actions.
>
> [Scroll down]
> Everyone sees the Upcoming Meetings card showing your next five
> scheduled meetings, and the Recent Minutes card with quick links to
> review past proceedings.
>
> Secretaries and chairs also see a 'Minutes Awaiting Approval' card —
> adjourned meetings whose minutes haven't been approved yet.
>
> If any board service terms are expiring within six months, you'll see
> an alert here too, so officers can plan ahead for elections.
>
> [Point to the nav bar]
> Use the navigation bar at the top to move around: Dashboard, Meetings,
> Governance, and — if you're an admin — Admin tools. There's also a
> Guide link that opens the built-in user manual."

# PART 2 — BEFORE THE MEETING

---

## Scene 4: Roles & Permissions Overview

**Duration:** ~2 minutes

### On Screen
1. Navigate to **Users** list (admin view) — show the role badges next to each name.
2. Display a table or overlay summarizing each role (can be a simple graphic
   matching the guide's role table).

### Narration

> "Before we start scheduling meetings, let's understand who does what.
> Quorum has roles that mirror the church's Bylaws.
>
> [Show role table or overlay]
>
> **Admin** — creates and manages user accounts, archives old items,
> and has access to the audit log. Admins can do everything a pastor can.
>
> **Pastor** — the president of the corporation. Chairs all meetings,
> schedules them, builds agendas, drives the live meeting, and approves
> minutes and reports.
>
> **Deacon** — serves on both the Board of Deacons and the Board of
> Administration. Advisory to the pastor. Votes on all board matters.
>
> **Trustee** — serves on the Board of Administration only. Custodian
> of church property. Votes on Board of Admin matters.
>
> **Secretary** — the record keeper. Takes roll call, builds agendas,
> edits minutes entries, and can run the entire live meeting on behalf
> of members who don't have devices.
>
> **Treasurer and Assistant Treasurer** — handle church finances and
> submit financial reports. Both serve on both boards.
>
> **Member** — a general church member. Can make motions, second
> motions, and cast votes at assembly meetings.
>
> One important detail: a user is considered 'voting' only if their
> profile has the Voting Member checkbox checked. An admin might be
> non-voting — for example, the pastor's assistant."

---

## Scene 5: User Management (Admin)

**Duration:** ~2 minutes

### On Screen
1. Navigate to **Admin → Users**.
2. Click **New user** — fill out the form (email, name, role, voting
   member checkbox, active member checkbox, family group).
3. Save the user.
4. Show the **Edit** button on an existing user — toggle role, reset password.
5. Show **Export CSV** and **Import CSV** buttons.

### Narration

> "If you're an admin, one of your first tasks is setting up user
> accounts. Go to Admin, then Users.
>
> [Click New User]
> Click New User. Fill in their email, full name, and pick their role
> from the dropdown. Check 'Voting member' if they can vote. Check
> 'Active member' if they're an active church member — this matters for
> assembly quorum calculations.
>
> The 'Family group' field tracks the constitutional rule that no two
> members of the same immediate family may serve on the same board.
>
> Set a temporary password and save. Tell the member their credentials
> so they can log in.
>
> [Show edit and password reset]
> You can edit any user later — change their role, toggle voting status,
> or reset their password.
>
> [Show CSV buttons]
> For bulk operations, use Export CSV to download the full user list as
> a spreadsheet. Make your changes — update roles, add new rows for new
> members — and then Import CSV to apply everything at once. New users
> need a password column with at least eight characters. Existing users
> matched by email are updated with any new values you provide.
>
> Every user management action is recorded in the admin action log, which
> we'll look at later."

---

## Scene 6: Scheduling a New Meeting

**Duration:** ~1 minute 30 seconds

### On Screen
1. Navigate to **Meetings → New Meeting**.
2. Fill in the form: meeting type dropdown (regular, special, annual,
   emergency), governing body dropdown (Board of Administration, Board
   of Deacons, Assembly), date/time, location.
3. Optionally show the **Acting chair** dropdown.
4. Save the meeting.
5. Show the meeting appear in the Meetings list and on the Dashboard.

### Narration

> "Let's schedule a meeting. Go to Meetings, then New Meeting.
>
> [Fill in the form]
> First, pick the meeting type. Most of your meetings will be 'Regular.'
> Annual business meetings, special sessions, and emergency meetings are
> also available.
>
> Next, select the governing body — Board of Administration, Board of
> Deacons, or Assembly. This is important because it determines who
> appears on the attendance roll and how quorum is calculated.
>
> Set the date, time, and location.
>
> [Point to Acting Chair dropdown]
> If the pastor won't be present, you can designate an Acting Chair.
> Per the Bylaws, the Board of Deacons selects a temporary chairman
> from its membership when the pastor is absent. The acting chair gets
> all chair powers for that one meeting — calling to order, advancing
> stages, opening and closing votes.
>
> Click Save, and you'll see the meeting appear on the Meetings list
> and on everyone's dashboard under Upcoming Meetings."

---

## Scene 7: Building the Agenda

**Duration:** ~2 minutes

### On Screen
1. Open the newly created meeting.
2. Click into the **Agenda** section.
3. Click **Add item** — fill in title, select category (Minutes approval,
   Report, Unfinished business, New business, Announcement), add a
   description, assign a presenter, check Confidential if needed.
4. Add 4–5 sample items to show variety.
5. Use the **arrow buttons** to reorder items.
6. Show the **Edit** button on an existing item.
7. Show the **Apply a template** dropdown (briefly — templates get their
   own scene later).

### Narration

> "Now let's build the agenda. Open your meeting and scroll to the
> Agenda section.
>
> [Click Add Item]
> Click Add Item. Give it a title — for example, 'Approve March minutes.'
> Pick a category. The categories follow Robert's Rules order of business:
>
> - **Minutes approval** — for approving the previous meeting's minutes.
> - **Report** — one item per committee or treasurer report.
> - **Unfinished business** — items carried over from last time.
> - **New business** — anything new requiring board action.
> - **Announcement** — FYI items that don't need a vote.
>
> You can add a description with extra details, assign a presenter if
> someone specific is walking the board through this item, and check
> 'Confidential' if the topic is sensitive — like staff salaries or
> personnel matters. We'll cover confidential minutes in more detail
> later.
>
> [Add several items, then reorder]
> Add as many items as you need. Use the arrow buttons to reorder them.
> Click Edit on any item to change its title, category, or presenter
> after the fact.
>
> [Show template dropdown]
> If your meetings follow a standard format, you can apply a saved
> template to pre-populate the agenda. We'll cover templates later in
> the video."

## Scene 8: Filing Reports & Attaching Documents

**Duration:** ~1 minute 30 seconds

### On Screen
1. Navigate to **Meetings → Reports → New Report**.
2. Fill in the report: title, select the meeting it's linked to, write
   content in markdown.
3. Save the report.
4. Go back to the meeting detail page — show the **Upload** button for
   attachments.
5. Upload a sample PDF. Show it appear in the attachments list.

### Narration

> "Before the meeting, the treasurer and committee chairs should file
> their reports. Go to Reports, then New Report.
>
> [Fill in the form]
> Give your report a title — for example, 'Treasurer Report — March 2026.'
> Link it to the meeting where it will be presented. Write your report
> content — the text field supports markdown for formatting.
>
> Save it, and the chair will approve it during the meeting.
>
> [Navigate to meeting, show attachments]
> You can also attach supporting documents to any meeting or agenda item.
> Back on the meeting detail page, click Upload. Attach the treasurer's
> spreadsheet, a proposed budget PDF, contractor bids — whatever the board
> needs to review.
>
> Allowed file types include PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, plain text,
> CSV, and images — up to twenty-five megabytes each. Every download is
> protected by login — no one can access files without being signed in,
> even if they have the direct link."

# PART 3 — RUNNING A LIVE MEETING

> **Production tip:** This is the most important part of the video. Consider
> using two browser windows side by side — one logged in as the Chair/Secretary
> and one as a board Member — so viewers can see both perspectives.

---

## Scene 9: Starting the Meeting — Call to Order

**Duration:** ~1 minute

### On Screen
1. Open the meeting detail page.
2. Click **Start Meeting**.
3. Show the status change to "In Progress" and the stage set to
   "Call to Order."
4. Point out the first minutes entry: "Chair called the meeting to
   order."
5. Click **Advance Stage** to move to "Roll Call."

### Narration

> "It's meeting time. Everyone logs in and opens the meeting page. The
> chair clicks Start Meeting.
>
> [Click Start Meeting]
> The status changes to 'In Progress' and the stage is set to 'Call to
> Order.' Look at the minutes timeline on the right — the very first
> entry is already there: 'Chair called the meeting to order' with a
> timestamp. That's the automatic minutes in action.
>
> [Click Advance Stage]
> The chair clicks Advance Stage to move to Roll Call. You'll move
> through stages in order throughout the meeting — the app follows
> Robert's Rules stage sequence automatically."

---

## Scene 10: Roll Call & the Quorum Badge

**Duration:** ~1 minute 30 seconds

### On Screen
1. Show the attendance panel on the live meeting page — a list of all
   members for this governing body.
2. The secretary clicks the **notification (!)** button next to each
   member to mark them as notified.
3. Toggle several members to **Present**.
4. Point out the **Quorum badge** in the top-left — show it turn from
   red to green when enough members are marked present.
5. Briefly show what happens if quorum is NOT met (red badge, warning).

### Narration

> "We're now at Roll Call. The attendance panel shows every member of
> this governing body.
>
> [Click notification buttons]
> First, the secretary marks each member as notified. For the Board of
> Deacons and Board of Administration, quorum requires that ALL members
> have been notified — the Bylaws are specific about this. The quorum
> badge will stay red until every member has a notification timestamp.
> Assembly meetings don't have this notification requirement in the
> quorum check.
>
> [Toggle members to Present]
> Now the secretary works down the list, toggling each member to Present
> as they arrive. Watch the quorum badge in the top-left corner...
>
> [Badge turns green]
> There — it turned green. We have quorum. For board meetings, quorum
> is a majority of members present. For assembly meetings, it's one-third
> of active members.
>
> If the badge is red, Robert's Rules says you cannot conduct substantive
> business. The body may only recess, set a future meeting date, or
> adjourn. The app enforces this — you'll see a warning if you try to
> proceed without quorum."

---

## Scene 11: Working Through the Agenda

**Duration:** ~1 minute 30 seconds

### On Screen
1. Advance to "Minutes Approval" stage — show the agenda panel with
   the Minutes Approval item.
2. Click **Take Up** on the agenda item — show it become bold.
3. Point out the minutes entry that appears.
4. Advance through stages: Reports → Unfinished Business → New Business.
5. Show the agenda items grouped under each stage.

### Narration

> "The chair advances through the stages one at a time. The agenda panel
> shows the items that belong to each stage.
>
> [Click Take Up]
> When you're ready to discuss an item, click 'Take Up.' Three things
> happen: the item's title goes bold so everyone can see what's being
> discussed, a timestamped minutes entry is created, and any motion made
> while this item is active gets automatically linked to it in the
> minutes.
>
> You can take up items in any order and revisit them later — just click
> Take Up again. There's no need to 'close' an item; simply take up the
> next one when the board is ready to move on.
>
> [Advance through stages]
> The stages follow Robert's Rules order of business: Minutes Approval,
> then Reports — where presenters walk the board through their filed
> reports. Then Unfinished Business — items carried over from last time.
> New Business — where most of the action happens. And finally
> Announcements — FYI items, no votes needed.
>
> Important: you cannot advance past a business stage while a motion is
> still on the floor. Quorum blocks the advance and shows a warning.
> You must dispose of the motion first — vote on it, withdraw it, or
> table it."

## Scene 12: Making & Seconding Motions

**Duration:** ~2 minutes

### On Screen
1. On the live meeting page (New Business stage), click **Make a Motion**.
2. Show the motion form: text field, motion type dropdown, vote method
   dropdown, "On behalf of" dropdown, "Deacons-only vote" checkbox.
3. Submit a motion — show it appear with status "Proposed."
4. A different member (or secretary on behalf) clicks **Second** — show
   the "Select who seconds" dropdown and "Second on behalf" button.
5. Status changes to "Seconded."
6. Briefly explain the secretary-driven workflow using the "On behalf of"
   feature.

### Narration

> "This is the heart of Robert's Rules. Let's make a motion.
>
> [Click Make a Motion]
> A voting member clicks 'Make a Motion.' Type the motion text — for
> example, 'I move that we approve the roof repair bid from ABC
> Contractors for twelve thousand dollars.'
>
> [Point to dropdowns]
> Select the motion type — most will be 'Main motion.' Some special types
> like Bylaw Amendment, Property Transfer, and Pastor Election
> automatically require a two-thirds supermajority vote and only appear
> during assembly meetings.
>
> Choose the vote method: 'Voice / show-of-hands' for when the chair
> counts aloud, or 'Roll call' for individual per-member voting through
> the app.
>
> [Point to 'On behalf of']
> Here's a powerful feature: the 'On behalf of' dropdown. If a board
> member doesn't have a device — maybe they're following along with a
> printed agenda — the secretary can make the motion on their behalf.
> Select the member who spoke, type their motion, and submit. The
> minutes will correctly attribute it to that member.
>
> [Submit the motion]
> The motion appears with a 'Proposed' badge.
>
> [Show seconding]
> Now a different voting member needs to second it. They can click
> 'I second the motion' on their own device, or the secretary can use
> the dropdown to select who seconded and click 'Second on behalf.'
> A maker cannot second their own motion — the app enforces this.
>
> The status changes to 'Seconded,' and the floor is open for debate.
> The chair manages debate verbally — there's no timer in the app."

## Scene 13: Voting — Voice and Roll Call

**Duration:** ~2 minutes 30 seconds

### On Screen
1. With a seconded motion on screen, the chair clicks **Open Vote** —
   status changes to "Voting."
2. **Voice vote demo:** Show the manual tally form — three fields for
   Yes, No, Abstain. Enter counts and submit.
3. Click **Close Vote** — show the result: "PASSED 4–1–1" with the
   status badge turning green.
4. Make a second motion with **Roll call** vote method selected.
5. Open the vote — show the roll-call grid with each present voting
   member listed with Yes / No / Abstain buttons.
6. Secretary fills in votes, clicks **Record All Votes**.
7. Show a member's view — they see their own Yes / No / Abstain buttons.
8. Close the vote — show the result with individual votes recorded.

### Narration

> "The motion has been seconded and debated. Time to vote. The chair
> clicks Open Vote.
>
> [Click Open Vote]
> The status changes to 'Voting.' What happens next depends on the vote
> method chosen when the motion was made.
>
> **Voice or show-of-hands vote:**
>
> [Show tally form]
> This is the simplest method. Members vote verbally or by raising hands.
> The chair or secretary counts and enters the totals — Yes, No, and
> Abstain — directly into the app. Individual members do NOT vote through
> the app for voice votes.
>
> This is the default and the right choice for most board motions, since
> only a few people typically have devices during the meeting.
>
> [Enter counts, close vote]
> Enter the counts and click Close Vote. The result is computed
> automatically — simple majority for most motions, two-thirds for
> supermajority types. A minutes entry is generated: 'Motion by Smith
> to approve the roof repair bid — PASSED 4–1–1, voice vote.'
>
> Note that abstentions never count toward the total. A motion passes
> simple majority if yes is greater than no.
>
> **Roll call vote:**
>
> [Show new motion with roll call]
> Now let's see a roll-call vote. When the vote opens, a grid appears
> showing every present voting member with Yes, No, and Abstain options.
>
> [Show secretary filling in grid]
> The secretary can record everyone's vote using this grid — click each
> member's choice and then 'Record All Votes.'
>
> [Switch to member view]
> Or, if members have their own devices, each one sees their own Yes,
> No, and Abstain buttons and votes individually. The tally updates in
> real time. Members can change their vote until the chair closes it.
>
> [Close vote]
> The chair closes the vote. The minutes show who voted which way,
> providing a full audit trail. The label says 'roll call' so the record
> is clear about which method was used."

## Scene 14: Amendments

**Duration:** ~1 minute 30 seconds

### On Screen
1. With a main motion on the floor (seconded, before vote), a member
   selects **Amendment** from the motion-type dropdown.
2. Submit the amendment — show it appear nested under the main motion.
3. Second the amendment.
4. Open vote on the amendment, vote, close — show it resolved.
5. Show the app "pop back" to the main motion for the final vote.

### Narration

> "Sometimes a member wants to change a motion before voting on it.
> That's an amendment.
>
> [Select Amendment from dropdown]
> While a main motion is on the floor, a member selects 'Amendment' from
> the motion-type dropdown and types the proposed change — for example,
> 'Amend to reduce the amount to ten thousand dollars.'
>
> [Submit and second]
> The amendment goes through its own full cycle: propose, second, debate,
> vote. It appears nested under the main motion so you can see the
> relationship.
>
> Robert's Rules says the innermost amendment is voted first. Quorum
> handles this automatically — the motion card always shows the deepest
> unresolved amendment as the active motion.
>
> [Vote on amendment]
> Let's vote on the amendment... it passed.
>
> [Show pop-back to main motion]
> Now the app pops back to the main motion — which may now be amended —
> for the final vote.
>
> One more thing: the maker of any motion can withdraw it before the vote
> opens. Withdrawn motions are kept in the minutes with a 'withdrawn'
> marker, so the record is complete."

## Scene 15: Adjournment

**Duration:** ~1 minute

### On Screen
1. Advance through remaining stages (Announcements).
2. Click **Adjourn**.
3. Show the status change to "Adjourned."
4. Show the final minutes entry: adjournment timestamp.
5. Show that the live page is now frozen — no more actions possible.

### Narration

> "We've worked through all the business. The chair advances to
> Announcements for any FYI items, then it's time to wrap up.
>
> [Click Adjourn]
> A member may formally move to adjourn, or in practice for a board
> meeting, the chair simply clicks Adjourn.
>
> The status changes to 'Adjourned,' the adjournment timestamp is logged
> in the minutes, and the meeting is frozen. No more actions can be taken
> on the live page.
>
> Look at the minutes timeline — it's the complete, automatic record of
> everything that happened: call to order, roll call, every agenda item
> taken up, every motion made, every vote cast, every note added, and
> adjournment. All timestamped, all in order."

# PART 4 — AFTER THE MEETING

---

## Scene 16: Reviewing & Editing Minutes

**Duration:** ~1 minute 30 seconds

### On Screen
1. Navigate to **Meetings → Minutes** — show the list of meetings
   with minutes.
2. Open the minutes for the meeting we just adjourned.
3. Show the timeline — reverse chronological, each entry with a
   timestamp and description.
4. Click **Edit** on a minutes entry — change wording, save.
5. Show the "edited" flag that appears on modified entries.
6. Show the chair/secretary adding a **Note** to the minutes.

### Narration

> "After the meeting, the secretary reviews the auto-generated minutes.
> Go to Meetings, then Minutes, and open the meeting.
>
> [Show timeline]
> The minutes timeline is reverse chronological — newest entries at the
> top. Every parliamentary action during the meeting generated an entry:
> motions, votes, stage changes, attendance — all timestamped
> automatically.
>
> [Click Edit on an entry]
> The secretary can edit any entry to fix wording, add clarity, or
> correct a name. Click Edit, make your change, and save. Edited entries
> get a small flag so the board knows a correction was made.
>
> [Add a note]
> You can also add notes for anything the app couldn't capture
> structurally — for example, 'Pastor joined by phone at 7:18 PM.'
> These become timestamped minutes entries just like everything else.
>
> All of this editing is available until the chair approves the minutes
> at the next meeting. After approval, the record is locked."

---

## Scene 17: Approving Minutes at the Next Meeting

**Duration:** ~1 minute

### On Screen
1. Show a new meeting in progress, at the "Minutes Approval" stage.
2. The agenda shows a "Minutes Approval" item for the previous meeting.
3. The chair clicks **Approve Minutes**.
4. Show the status change — minutes are now locked.
5. Show that the Edit buttons are gone on the approved minutes.

### Narration

> "Minutes are formally approved at the next meeting. When you create an
> agenda item with the 'Minutes Approval' category, it links to the
